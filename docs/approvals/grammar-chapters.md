# Grammar chapters: for your approval

Status: **waiting for the learner's approval.** Nothing here is final until you say so. The integration branch is not merged to `main` before that.

This document lists every grammar chapter of the tree, level by level, in the order the app walks through them. It belongs to ticket #6 ("All 212 grammar topics in the tree").

## What you are asked to check

1. **The chapter names**, in English, Persian and simple German.
2. **The order of the chapters** inside each level. The app's "next topic" follows this order: all topics of chapter 1, then all topics of chapter 2, and so on.
3. **Which topics are in which chapter.**
4. **The judgment calls** at the end. Each one is a decision I made alone and you may want differently.

To change something, say it in plain words: "rename A1.7 to …", "move A2.10 before A2.9", "move topic 85 to A1.4", "join B1.8 and B1.9". Moving a chapter is a one-line change; nothing else has to be rewritten.

## How the chapters were made

- The 212 topics of the approved grammar list are in **41 chapters**: 15 in A1 (85 topics), 12 in A2 (55 topics), 14 in B1 (72 topics).
- A chapter is a group of grammar topics of one level about one subject. The chapters follow the sections of the grammar list ("Verbs and tenses", "Prepositions", …). A long section is split into two or three chapters; a section with only one or two topics in a level is joined to a neighbour.
- **Inside a chapter, the topics keep the order of the grammar list.** The rows that were added later (204 to 212) stand at their teaching positions, as the list's changelog says, not at the end.
- **The order of the chapters inside a level is a teaching order**, not the order of the list's sections. A chapter comes after the chapters it builds on. For example, in A1 "The cases" comes after "Nouns and articles", and "Talking about the past" comes late.
- The number before a topic is its number in the grammar list. The app's identifier is the same number: topic 97 is `grammar-097`.
- **The Persian names are a draft written by an AI.** A native reader reviews all Persian text later (ticket #34). The German names are simple German, also a draft.
- German grammar words that you will meet in every German course (Akkusativ, Dativ, Konjunktiv II, Perfekt) stay German in all three languages.

## Judgment calls

Each of these is a decision the grammar list does not make. Tell me which ones to change.

1. **Small chapters.** The mock-up showed a chapter with 16 topics. These chapters have 3 to 10 topics, so that a chapter has one clear subject and is finished sooner. The price is more chapters per level (15, 12 and 14), and the vocabulary and phrase chapters will be added between them later. If you prefer fewer and bigger chapters, these neighbours join well: A1.1 with A1.2, A1.3 with A1.4, B1.2 with B1.3 ("Subordinate clauses", 13 topics) and B1.4 with B1.5 ("Connectors", 11 topics).

2. **Chapter order is a teaching order.** The grammar list is ordered by subject (all verbs, then all modal verbs, …), which is not an order to learn in: the Perfekt would come before the articles. I ordered the chapters of each level so that each one builds on earlier ones. This order is my proposal; the list does not give one. Places where you may disagree:
   - A1 starts with "Verbs in the present" (4 topics) and then "First sentences and questions". Separable verbs and the imperative come later, in A1.8.
   - A1.11 "Pronouns" (mich, dich, mir, dir, man …) comes after "Prepositions", in the second half of A1, as in most coursebooks.
   - A1.14 "Building sentences" (Satzklammer; und, oder, aber, denn; dann, zuerst) is late in A1. You will meet "und" and "aber" in examples long before; the chapter is where they are explained.
   - A2 puts the passive (A2.10) and relative clauses (A2.11) late, as the list recommends ("late A2").
   - B1 starts with the tenses and the time clauses, because "nachdem" needs the Plusquamperfekt. The passive is late in B1.

3. **Topics that left their section of the list.** Most chapters are a piece of one section. These topics moved, because they fit another chapter better in their level:
   - 81 (personal pronouns) and 87 (W-question words): from "Articles and pronouns" to A1.2 "First sentences and questions".
   - 74, 75, 76 (ein, no article, kein): together with 59, 205, 60 in A1.3 "Nouns and articles".
   - 46 (geöffnet, geschlossen): from "Passive" to A1.12 "Talking about the past", after the Partizip II.
   - 52 (infinitive as an instruction): from "Infinitive constructions" to A1.8, after the imperative.
   - 34, 35 (möchte, würde gern): from "Konjunktiv II" to A1.9 "Modal verbs and polite wishes".
   - 21, 22, 47 (werden, lassen, Passiv Präsens): one small chapter, A2.10, because the passive is built with werden.
   - 151, 207, 194 (deshalb and sonst; schon and noch; montags): one small chapter of adverbs, A2.8.
   - 199, 200, 202 (verb prefixes, -chen, the particles doch, mal, denn): A2.12.
   - 32, 33 (brauchen + zu; modal verbs for assumption): with the Konjunktiv II in B1.12, after the chapter on the infinitive with zu.
   - 195, 201, 203 (fractions; -heit, -keit, -ig, -lich; modal particles): one mixed closing chapter, B1.14. Each was alone in its section.

4. **Topics with two levels in the list.** The list says a topic's level is "where the topic is first introduced". I followed that:
   - 123 (Woher?: aus; von), "A1 / A2": in A1.
   - 204 (verbs with fixed prepositions), "A2 (core set) / B1 (full list)": in A2, directly before 97, as the list asks.
   - 197 (-in, -er, -ung), "A1 (rec.) / A2 (active)", and 198 (un-, -los, -bar), "A1 (rec.) / A2": in A1, in the last chapter. They are **not** marked recognise-only, because you must be able to use them by B1. The other choice is to put both in A2.

5. **"A1 (late)" topics.** The list marks 31, 80, 83, 105, 120, 128 and 210 as late A1. Six of them are in the second half of A1. Topic 80 (welch-) is in A1.7 with the other article words, which is in the first half.

6. **Topic 210 "before 199".** The list puts 210 directly before 199, but 210 is an A1 topic and 199 an A2 topic. So 210 is the last word-building topic of A1 (the last topic of A1), and 199 is the first topic of the A2 word-building chapter.

7. **Recognise-only.** Six topics carry the flag: 33, 51, 52, 118, 176 and 183. These are exactly the rows that the list marks "rec." in its Level column. The list's notes also call a few other topics "receptive" or "mainly receptive" without marking the row: 49 (Passiv Perfekt), 116 (adjective endings in the Genitiv) and 203 (modal particles, "mostly for understanding"). They are not flagged. Say so if they should be.

8. **Prerequisites.** The grammar list has no prerequisites, so I wrote them: one or two per topic for most, none for three topics that start from nothing (1, 59 and 190). Every prerequisite is an earlier topic in the order above, so there are no circles and "next topic" never asks for something you have not seen. Prerequisites only advise; they never lock a topic. They are not listed here, to keep this document short; each topic's file in `content/nodes/grammar/` has them.

9. **Topic titles.** The English titles are the titles of the grammar list, word for word. The Persian and simple German titles of the 212 topics are drafts, like the chapter names. They are not listed here; you can read them in the app by switching the language, and the native reader of ticket #34 reviews the Persian ones.

10. **One topic is already written.** Topic 1 (Präsens, regular conjugation) was published with the first version of the app. All 211 others show "Coming soon".
